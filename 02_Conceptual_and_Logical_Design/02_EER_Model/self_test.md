# Self-Test — EER Model

**Q1. What is the difference between ER and EER models?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
The EER model extends the ER model by adding specialization, generalization, inheritance, and more advanced entity relationships.
</details>

**Q2. What is a superclass?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
A superclass is a general entity that contains attributes common to multiple subclasses.
</details>

**Q3. What is a subclass?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
A subclass is a specialized entity that inherits from a superclass and may add extra attributes.
</details>

**Q4. What does specialization mean?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Specialization is the process of breaking a general entity into more specific entity types.
</details>

**Q5. What does generalization mean?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Generalization is the process of combining several similar entities into one broader entity.
</details>

**Q6. What is the difference between disjoint and overlapping specialization?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Disjoint means an instance belongs to only one subclass; overlapping means an instance may belong to more than one subclass.
</details>

**Q7. What is total specialization?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Every superclass instance must belong to at least one subclass.
</details>

**Q8. Why is EER useful in real systems?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Because many systems naturally involve role hierarchies, inherited attributes, and multiple business subtypes.
</details>

---

## Quick revision

- EER = ER + inheritance + specialization
- Superclass = general type
- Subclass = specialized type
- Disjoint vs overlapping = exclusive or shared membership
- Total vs partial = required or optional subclass membership
