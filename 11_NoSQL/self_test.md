# Self-Test — NoSQL

**Q1. What does NoSQL generally mean?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
It generally refers to databases that do not use the traditional relational model, or “not only SQL” systems.
</details>

**Q2. What are the most common NoSQL data models?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Document, key-value, column-family, and graph databases.
</details>

**Q3. Why do organizations use NoSQL systems?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
For flexibility, high-scale workloads, distributed systems, and large semi-structured datasets.
</details>

**Q4. What does ACID mean?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability; a relational database property emphasizing strong guarantees.
</details>

**Q5. What does BASE mean?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
Basically Available, Soft state, Eventual consistency; a common NoSQL trade-off model.
</details>

**Q6. When is a relational database usually a better choice than NoSQL?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
When data is structured, integrity rules are critical, and you need complex joins and strong transactional guarantees.
</details>

**Q7. When is NoSQL usually a better choice?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
When the data is flexible, high-volume, distributed, or rapidly changing.
</details>

**Q8. What is a document database?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
A database that stores data as documents, usually structured as JSON-like objects.
</details>

---

## Quick revision

- NoSQL = flexible alternative database model
- Common models: document, key-value, column-family, graph
- Relational = structured and consistent
- NoSQL = scalable and flexible
- Choice depends on workload and business requirements
